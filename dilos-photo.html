How It Works

Three steps. You approve the last one.

Archiving runs on its own in the background. Removing from iCloud only happens when you click Remove and confirm.

STEP 1

Set your rules

Decide how long photos stay in iCloud: the last two years, Favorites forever, a Travel album for five years, screenshots for three months.

STEP 2

Archived automatically

Photos past their rules are copied to your folder in full quality, each copy checked against the original. Your library stays untouched.

STEP 3

Free up iCloud, when ready

See exactly what's archived and how much space you'll get back. Click Remove, confirm with macOS, and the photos go to Recently Deleted.

Retention Rules

Your rules decide what stays in iCloud

Set one default, then add exceptions for what matters to you. When several rules apply to a photo, the most protective one wins, so a favorite in a short-lived album is still kept.

  • Rules for Favorites, any album, and smart albums like Screenshots, Videos or Selfies
  • Album rules survive renames and tell apart albums with the same name
  • Change a rule anytime; photos it now protects simply stay in iCloud

Built Carefully

Made for photos you can't replace

Every step is designed so nothing leaves iCloud until a verified copy is safe on your drive.

Verified copies

Every archived file is checked against the original's size and opened to confirm it's a readable image or video before the photo counts as archived.

Removal is always yours

Nothing is ever deleted automatically. You review, click Remove, and confirm in macOS's own dialog. Removed photos wait in Recently Deleted.

Full quality, nothing lost

Originals in their own format (HEIC, RAW, ProRes), plus your edits and the video half of Live Photos. Bursts are kept together.

An archive you can read

Plain files in year and month folders, each with a small details file: date, location, albums and favorite. No database, no lock-in.

Hidden stays hidden

Photos in your Hidden album are never archived or removed. Edited a photo after it was archived? It's archived again first.

Quietly in the menu bar

Checks for library changes on your schedule, pauses on battery if you like, and stops before your archive drive fills up.

Your Archive

Your photos, on a drive you own

Choose any folder: on your Mac, an external drive or a NAS. Dilos Photo files everything by the month it was taken, so finding a photo from a summer five years ago takes seconds in Finder.

Each file keeps its capture date, so it sorts correctly anywhere. Back up the folder like any other, with Time Machine or your favorite tool.

Your photos never leave your Mac

Dilos Photo works entirely on your Mac. It has no account, no servers and no analytics. Your photos move between iCloud Photos, which Apple runs, and the folder you choose. Nowhere else.

Questions

Good to know

Can't find your answer? Ask in the form below.

Is it safe to remove photos from iCloud?

Dilos Photo offers a photo for removal only after its archived copy has been written and verified. When you remove, macOS asks you to confirm, and the photos go to Recently Deleted in Photos, where they can usually be recovered for 30 days. Keep your archive folder backed up: once photos are removed from iCloud, it holds their copies.

Will removed photos disappear from my iPhone too?

Yes. Removing from iCloud Photos removes the photo from every device using that library. That's what frees the iCloud space. The archived copy stays in your folder on your Mac or drive.

Do I need an external drive?

No. Any folder works, including one on your Mac's internal disk. An external drive or NAS is a good choice for large libraries. Avoid folders that sync to iCloud Drive, since the archive would use iCloud space again; Dilos Photo warns you if you pick one.

What about edited photos, Live Photos and bursts?

Edited photos are archived with both the original and your edited version. Live Photos keep their video. Bursts are archived and removed as one, never partially. If you edit a photo after it was archived, it's archived again before it can be removed.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel, using iCloud Photos as its System Photo Library.
